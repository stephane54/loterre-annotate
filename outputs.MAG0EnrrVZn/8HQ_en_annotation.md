## Texte annoté

The term chemical element is used in two different but closely related meanings: it can mean a chemical substance consisting of a single kind of atoms (a free element), or it can mean that kind of atoms as a component of various chemical substances. For example, molecules of water (H2O) contain atoms of **hydrogen**〔[hydrogen](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/8HQ-DTLN6DML-5)〕 (H) and **oxygen**〔[oxygen](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/8HQ-RX5D8P7R-1)〕 (O), so water can be said as a compound consisting of the elements **hydrogen**〔[hydrogen](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/8HQ-DTLN6DML-5)〕 (H) and **oxygen**〔[oxygen](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/8HQ-RX5D8P7R-1)〕 (O) even though it does not contain the chemical substances (di)hydrogen (H2) and (di)oxygen (O2), as H2O molecules are different from H2 and O2 molecules. For the meaning chemical substance consisting of a single kind of atoms, the terms elementary substance and simple substance have been suggested, but they have not gained much acceptance in English chemical literature, whereas in some other languages their equivalent is widely used. For example, the French chemical terminology distinguishes élément chimique (kind of atoms) and corps simple (chemical substance consisting of a single kind of atoms)
