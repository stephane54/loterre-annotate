The word Parthenon comes from the Greek parthénos (παρθένος) 'maiden, girl' as well as 'virgin, unmarried **woman**〔[woman](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-TVVBD8BG-5)〕'. The Liddell–Scott–Jones Greek–English Lexicon states that it may have referred to the unmarried **women**〔[woman](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-TVVBD8BG-5)〕's apartments in a **house**〔[house](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-GV4CX8CG-M)〕, but that in the Parthenon it seems to have been used for a particular room of the temple.[17] There is some debate as to which room that was. The lexicon **states**〔[state](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-SHRC9CFK-T)〕 that this room was the western cella of the Parthenon. This has also been suggested by J.B. Bury.[10] One **theory**〔[theory](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-D8QQW1F8-X)〕 is that the Parthenon was the room where the arrephoroi, a group of four young girls chosen to serve Athena each year, wove a peplos that was presented to Athena during Panathenaic Festivals.[18] Christopher Pelling asserts that the **name**〔[name](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-CKZMHMGN-T)〕 Parthenon means the **temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕 of the virgin **goddess**〔[goddess](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-T2CGDKX6-G)〕, referring to the **cult**〔[cult](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-TC5WRVFF-6)〕 of Athena Parthenos that was associated with the temple.[19] It has also been suggested that the **name**〔[name](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-CKZMHMGN-T)〕 of the **temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕 alludes to the maidens (parthénoi), whose supreme **sacrifice**〔[sacrifice](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-N9NGSTZL-P)〕 guaranteed the safety of the city.[20] In that case, the room originally known as the Parthenon could have been a part of the **temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕 known today as the Erechtheion. In 5th-century BC **accounts**〔[accounts](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-F5CC0WWZ-R)〕 of the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕, the structure is simply called ὁ νᾱός (ho **naos**〔[naos](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-CKM6FZT2-J)〕; lit. the **temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕). Douglas Frame writes that the **name**〔[name](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-CKZMHMGN-T)〕 Parthenon was a nickname related to the **statue**〔[statue](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-ZJH0JC27-Q)〕 of Athena Parthenos, and only appeared a century after construction. He contends that Athena's **temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕 was never officially called the Parthenon and she herself most likely never had the **cult**〔[cult](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-TC5WRVFF-6)〕 title parthénos.[22] The ancient **architects**〔[architect](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-PR8F63CH-X)〕 Iktinos and Callicrates appear to have called the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 Ἑκατόμπεδος (Hekatómpedos; lit. the hundred footer) in their lost **treatise**〔[treatise](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-R34595CH-3)〕 on Athenian architecture.[23] Harpocration wrote that some people used to call the Parthenon the Hekatompedos, not due to its size but because of its beauty and fine proportions.[23] The first instance in which Parthenon definitely refers to the entire **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 comes from the fourth century BC orator Demosthenes.[24] In the **4th century BC**〔[4th century BC](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-DJKPXL7G-L)〕 and later, the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 was referred to as the Hekatompedos or the Hekatompedon as well as the Parthenon. Plutarch referred to the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 during the first century AD as the Hekatompedos Parthenon.A 2020 study by Janric van Rookhuijzen supports the idea that the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 known today as the Parthenon was originally called the Hekatompedon. Based on literary and historical research, he proposes that the treasury called the Parthenon should be recognized as the west part of the **building**〔[building](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-KFCR868K-J)〕 now conventionally known as the Erechtheion.Because the Parthenon was dedicated to the Greek **goddess**〔[goddess](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-T2CGDKX6-G)〕 Athena, it was sometimes called the **Temple**〔[temple](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-RQSRPJCL-R)〕 of Minerva, the Roman **name**〔[name](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-CKZMHMGN-T)〕 for Athena, particularly during the 19th century. Parthénos was also applied to the Virgin Mary (Parthénos Maria) when the Parthenon was converted to a Christian **church**〔[church](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/27X-Q4FH9ZN2-S)〕 dedicated to the Virgin Mary in the final decade of the 6th century.
