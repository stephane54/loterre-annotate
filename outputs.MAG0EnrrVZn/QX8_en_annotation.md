## Texte annoté

The Tethysides are a superorogenic complex flanking the Eurasian continent to the south and consisting of the Cimmerides and Alpides, products of Palaeo- and Neo-**Tethys**〔[Tethys](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8Z8RC09C-B)〕 respectively. We here review their evolution, mainly on the basis of new maps showing the distribution of sutures, **magmatic rocks**〔[magmatic rock](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-1DC2HQDP-3)〕, certain palaeobiogeographically and palaeoclimatologically significant **taxa**〔[taxon](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-KXPM460G-Q)〕 and **facies**〔[facies](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-RXQMRH14-H)〕, and fragments of Pan-African (900–450 Ma) orogenic system forming the basement of many Tethyside blocks. These are supplemented by palaeomagnetic data reported in the literature. A fundamental tenet of this paper is that major sutures which contain ophiolite fragments, represent **tectonic**〔[tectonic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-JVN4KQ81-5)〕 sections between continental blocks where oceanic crust has been subducted. Palaeo-**Tethys**〔[Tethys](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8Z8RC09C-B)〕 came into existence largely in late **Carboniferous**〔[Carboniferous](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-M45C3M72-0)〕 time. Coevally, it began to be consumed by both internal and peripheral subduction zones, which continued into the **Permian**〔[Permian](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-03QFNT2Q-B)〕; some of these had been inherited from pre-Tethyan times. In the later **Permian**〔[Permian](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-03QFNT2Q-B)〕, rifting subparallel with the northern margin of **Gondwana**〔[Gondwana](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3DNKWZGQ-0)〕 Land began between the Zagros and Malaysia, separating a Cimmerian continent from **N.**〔[nitrogen](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8BD2JWLH-G)〕 **Gondwana**〔[Gondwana](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3DNKWZGQ-0)〕 Land, and thus heralding the opening of Neo-**Tethys**〔[Tethys](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8Z8RC09C-B)〕 and other smaller **oceans**〔[ocean](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-6VDMXQ58-M)〕 that were **back-arc basins**〔[back-arc](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-6FDWFKH9-6)〕 of Palaeo-**Tethys**〔[Tethys](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8Z8RC09C-B)〕. This rifting possibly also extended farther west into Crete and mainland Greece. However, the North China block, Yangtze block, Huanan block, the eastern moity of the Qangtang block (North Tibet), and Annamia, all originally pieces of the end-**Proterozoic**〔[Proterozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-6V9LHWZ0-K)〕-early **Palaeozoic**〔[Paleozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-9D67BWNK-P)〕 **Gondwana**〔[Gondwana](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3DNKWZGQ-0)〕 Land, had already separated from it in pre-late **Carboniferous**〔[Carboniferous](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-M45C3M72-0)〕 times, possibly during the **Devonian**〔[Devonian](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-XWH1GP6V-1)〕. All of these blocks, and the Cimmerian continent, were characterized by Cathaysian floral elements in late **Palaeozoic**〔[Paleozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-9D67BWNK-P)〕 time. Palaeomagnetic and palaeontological data showing the original **Gondwana**〔[Gondwana](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3DNKWZGQ-0)〕 Land affinity of these continental blocks are supplemented by correlating late **Proterozoic**〔[Proterozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-6V9LHWZ0-K)〕-early **Palaeozoic**〔[Paleozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-9D67BWNK-P)〕 Pan-African sutures, orogenic belts, and **sedimentary basin**〔[sedimentary basin](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-T89Q2V2C-4)〕 fragments across Tethyside sutures. Late **Permian**〔[Permian](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-03QFNT2Q-B)〕 foraminiferal provinces are related to this palaeogeographical interpretation. By **Triassic**〔[Triassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-WNRCPDN4-F)〕 times, most Cimmeride subduction zones were already in existence. The Cimmerian Continent accelerated its separation from **Gondwana**〔[Gondwana](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3DNKWZGQ-0)〕 Land and—locally in the late **Permian**〔[Permian](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-03QFNT2Q-B)〕—began disintegrating internally along the Waser/Rushan-Pshart/Banggong Co-Nu Jiang/Mandalay **ocean**〔[ocean](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-6VDMXQ58-M)〕. By late **Triassic**〔[Triassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-WNRCPDN4-F)〕 time all of the Chinese blocks—except Lhasa-and Annamia had collided with each other and with Laurasia. The resulting enormous orogenic collage had a ‘soft cushion’ between itself and Laurasia, in the form of the enormous accretionary complex of the Songpan-Ganzi. This connection enabled Laurasian land vertebrates to reach south-east Asia by late **Triassic**〔[Triassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-WNRCPDN4-F)〕 time. In late **Triassic**〔[Triassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-WNRCPDN4-F)〕 to **middle Jurassic**〔[Middle Jurassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-354H97JN-V)〕 times, most major Cimmeride collisions were completed. Widespread **aridity**〔[aridity](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-W1XPBFWP-6)〕 in Central Asia occurred in **late Jurassic**〔[Upper Jurassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-8P2MCXF1-L)〕 time, probably in the **rain**〔[rainfall](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-L5TZ9NS8-J)〕 shadow of the newly formed Cimmeride mountain wall. Neo-Tethyan subduction systems formed along the S. margin of the Cimmerides or within Neo-Tethyan oceanic **lithosphere**〔[lithosphere](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-ZBQKL696-6)〕 during the **Jurassic**〔[Jurassic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-F2MQ8RWG-H)〕. Most, if not all, were north- or east-dipping. They continued the northerly **migration**〔[migration](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-2NBPVFSX-L)〕 of the Tethyside blocks. Evolution of the Tethysides influenced the distribution of marine and terrestrial organisms, and affected **sea-level**〔[sea level](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3TBL5X6C-6)〕 changes and patterns of **atmospheric circulation**〔[atmospheric circulation](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-1ZVLF956-7)〕 during much of the **Mesozoic**〔[Mesozoic](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-13GXZD3Q-1)〕 and Cainozoic. It is likely to have reflected the surface expression of a persistent trend in the large-scale convective circulation in the **mantle**〔[mantle](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-P8MWMXWC-D)〕, that continuously transported material northward into the Tethyan domain. The **climate**〔[climate](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-CP0QNVH1-X)〕 of the early Earth was probably very warm and has, in general, reduced since the **Archean**〔[Archean](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-5JG3C031-Z)〕. However, it now seems that the world is about 0.6°C warmer than it was 100 years ago and estimates of the rate of **global warming**〔[global warming](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-RNHMZ6CJ-2)〕 over the next century range from 0.16°C to 0.35°C per decade. Concurrently, global **sea-level**〔[sea level](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-3TBL5X6C-6)〕 is predicted to rise from 2.4 to 10 cm per decade. These rates of change are much faster than those normally associated with the **geological record**〔[geological record](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-DX01Q2F7-1)〕, causing geologists and palaeontologists to reassess their data and their forecasts on rates of future change. With the current interest in global **climatic change**〔[climate change](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-QLSHDGTM-R)〕 and, more specifically, with **global warming**〔[global warming](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-RNHMZ6CJ-2)〕, it is clear that palaeontologists have valuable information to provide on the impacts of past **climatic change**〔[climate change](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-QLSHDGTM-R)〕. This volume contains papers from an international array of such geologists and palaeontologists, showing how studies of micro- and macrofossils, plant and vertebrate **fossils**〔[fossil](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-625M7DXH-J)〕 from a range of **geological ages**〔[geological age](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-SN2DMGJG-M)〕 have contributed to our understanding of how **climate**〔[climate](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-CP0QNVH1-X)〕 affects both local and more widespread areas. The contributions are arranged in geological order, ranging from the Permo-Carboniferous to the post-**glacial**〔[glaciation](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-5K8FD2VC-9)〕 recovery of the last 18,000 years, with an emphasis on **climate change**〔[climate change](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-QLSHDGTM-R)〕 during the last two million years, particularly in NW **Europe**〔[Europe](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-H5SN0WKR-B)〕. **Climates**〔[climate](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-CP0QNVH1-X)〕: Past and Present will be of interest to palaeontologists, geologists and palaeoclimatologists who specialize in climatic reconstructions and any professionals enagaged in research into the geological aspects of **climate change**〔[climate change](http://data.loterre.fr/ark:/67375/QX8-QLSHDGTM-R)〕.
